available production (lot) codes and/or dates; an overall view of the lot(s) examined; and any adverse conditions

Photographic evidence must be captured with a government-issued digital device and copied to your work computer’s

by simply including the remarks: “information panel/right side, 1 of 4.”
Photographs may also be downloaded and combined into a single document (Word or PDF) for each entry/line, with
the required information included within the document.
If photographic evidence is printed, it should be documented in your regulatory notes and the required information
must be permanently affixed to the printed photos so that there is never any loss of association between the
photographic evidence and the associated entry/line. This information must be recorded immediately above or below
the photograph(s) (not directly on the photos) so that the integrity of the evidence is not compromised.
Note: If additional enforcement or legal actions, including, but not limited to, seizure, injunction, debarment, or
prosecution are contemplated with respect to an import case, the procedures for preparing and maintaining digital
photographs and video as evidence, as described in IOM 5.6.7 and IOM 5.7.3, must be followed.

6.2.2 – Initial Entry Review
The FDA receives electronically submitted lines with one of two initial work types:
•
Quantity and Value (QAV)
•
No Quantity and Value (NQV)
To aid in the admissibility process, filers typically provide the quantity and value for each line electronically. Both are
required to set up a work request. For paper (non-ABI) entries, apply the same decision-making criteria as you would
for electronic entries.
NOTE: For instructions on creating a manual entry in the Entry Review (ER) application for a non-ABI entry, please
consult the ER Job Aid.
To prioritize lines for review, use the following date and time information:
•
Trucks: Actual arrival date and time.
•
Air, Rail, and Sea: Submission date and time.
In general, prioritize lines as follows:
[p.16]









1. Lines with a QAV work type have priority over those with an NQV work type.
2. Lines with documents submitted through the Import Trade Auxiliary Communications System (ITACS) take
priority over lines with documents sent by other methods.
6.2.2.1 –Emergency and Perishable Shipments
Emergency and perishable products are time sensitive and take precedence over all others.
Emergency products are when one or more items need immediate review due to an urgent situation. Import
divisions decide how to handle these entries to prevent abuse.
Perishable products are items that can spoil or lose quality if stored too long under normal conditions. Examples
include:
•
Raw/fresh seafood
•
Fresh fruits and vegetables
The Centers identify time-sensitive products to help divisions prioritize their reviews.
Release device shipments only if the entry documents show CDRH approval. If you can't verify the approval
document, contact CDRH at cdrhimport@fda.hhs.gov.
6.2.2.2 – Reviewing Entry Data and Information
When the FDA's initial automated screening does not issue a "May Proceed," an entry reviewer must manually
review it.
You will use the FDA Import Systems to conduct your review. This system includes PREDICT, a screening tool that
helps identify high-risk shipments. PREDICT does this by analyzing:
•
The product's known risks.
•
The history of the importer, manufacturer, and shipper.
The system then flags the highest-risk lines for your review.
Before you take any action, you must review and consider the recommendations from the FDA Import System,
including the PREDICT risk score and any rule flags.
Priority Review Flags
A "Priority Review" flag points to a major public safety risk. This includes products that are subject to Detention
Without Physical Examination (DWPE).
If you decide to issue a "May Proceed" for a line with a priority review flag, you must write a clear reason in the
"Remarks" field. Vague remarks do not provide enough information.
Examples of Clear Remarks:
•
“The product brand submitted for entry is not a brand name subject to DWPE.”
•
“Product is in powdered rather than liquid form.”
•
“Specific manufacturer and/or product is exempt from IA XX-XX.”
•
“Documentation shows imported item does not contain heparin.”
Examples of Vague Remarks:
•
“Line is not subject to IA”
•
“NA”
•
“May Proceeded”
Before deciding, you must perform the following activities:
•
Analyze Risk: Review the PREDICT risk score and all flags in the FDA Import Systems.
[p.17]









•
Investigate Alerts: If you see an Import Alert (IA) flag, confirm if the firm or product is subject to DWPE.
If you believe a flag is missing, investigate it.
•
Report Errors: If a PREDICT rule works incorrectly, report it using the system's feedback function.
•
Research: Search for the firm and product in the relevant databases and review entry documents as
needed.
•
Request Field Work: Set-up field work based on current plans, assignments, and obligations.
•
Follow Guidance: Use the correct guidance documents to check for compliance.
As an entry reviewer, you must be familiar with the following resources:
•
Commodity-Specific Information: Find product-specific details and contacts on the Import Program
page.
•
Compliance Manuals and Guides: Use these to help you evaluate if a product follows FDA laws. This
includes Compliance Program Guidance Manuals, the PREDICT Guide for Rules and Scoring, and
Chapter 9 of the RPM.
•
Internal Documents: Refer to the OII Field Work Plan, SCOPE, active assignments, and other internal
notices and procedures.
•
Affirmations of Compliance (A of C): Understand the definitions for the codes used with entries.
•
The most current FDA Supplemental Guide for specific ACE submission requirements and mandatory
data elements
•
Regulatory Procedure Manual (RPM) Chapter 9 and the OIO SharePoint site for additional information.

6.2.2.2.1 – Manual “May Proceed”
 Issuing a "May Proceed"
You should issue a "May Proceed" for an entry line if you can confirm it meets all regulatory requirements
using the electronic information and documents, and you see no need to detain it or request field work.
NOTE: If the system’s automated check clears the line, do not manually verify the Affirmation of Compliance (A
of C) data.
The "May Proceed" Notice
The FDA automatically sends a "May Proceed Notice" to both Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and the
filer. The filer is then responsible for telling the importer. This notice means the shipment can continue
without further FDA examination.
A shipment can receive a "May Proceed" notice in two ways:
1. It passes the FDA's initial automated screening.
2. An entry reviewer manually issues a “May Proceed” it after review.
Important: A product that is allowed to enter the country can still face legal action later if the FDA finds it
violates the law (as stated in section 304(d) of the FD&C Act).
6.2.2.2.2 – Rescinding a “May Proceed”
You should only rescind a "May Proceed" in rare cases, such as when a product requires a compliance action.
You must act immediately, as this action is not for routine examinations.
Once you issue a "May Proceed," the conditional release period for that entry ends permanently. Even if you
rescind the notice, the period does not reopen.
Steps to Rescind a "May Proceed"
If a "May Proceed" was issued in error or new information requires another review, follow these steps:
1. Get your supervisor's approval before taking any other action.
[p.18]









2. Notify the filer immediately that you rescinded the "May Proceed" and are holding the line for a new
admissibility decision.
3. Send an updated "Notice of FDA Action" to the filer, importer, and consignee within 24 hours.
4. Request a bond hold from CBP within 24 hours to protect against financial loss if the product is
refused.
5. Confirm the system sends the ABI codes to the filer, which tells them not to distribute the product.
6. Instruct CBP to demand redelivery if the shipment has already been distributed. Act quickly, as CBP has
a 30-day window to issue this demand.
7. Document all your actions and communications in the FDA's Import system to maintain a clear record.
6.2.2.2.3 – Request Examination and/or Sampling (LEX/FEX/SAM)
As an entry reviewer, follow these steps when you need to request a field examination or sample collection.
1. Correct Inaccurate Data
If you find errors in the transmitted data that could affect the admissibility decision, correct them first. Record
the reason for the update, save your changes, and then rescreen the data.
2. Set Up the Work Request
Next, set up the examination or sample collection using the correct work type and Problem Area Flag (PAF).
Align the work request with the agency's current priorities, work plans, and assignments.
3. Provide Clear Instructions
For any non-routine work, you must give the investigator clear directions in the "Instruction Text" field.
Provide instructions when:
•
An OII assignment requires specific remarks.
•
The exam requires special instructions (e.g., following a specific procedure guide).
•
You found a specific issue during your review that needs to be checked.
•
You need to reference a previous violation (include the entry/sample number).
•
An Import Bulletin directs you to do so.
•
You need to note safety precautions or other important product details.
Important Notes
•
Import Alerts (IA): Do not routinely request work for a line that is already subject to an Import Alert.
•
Job Aid: For more detailed instructions on these steps, refer to the Job Aid for the Entry
Review Application.
6.2.2.2.4 – Notices
When you recommend detention or request field work, the FDA's Import systems automatically notify the filer
in two ways:
•
Through the Automated Broker Interface (ABI).
•
With a "Notice of FDA Action."
Distribute Notices of FDA Action per IOM 6.1.6.2. You can also find additional information regarding the

Entry reviewers can identify CBP cancelled entries in FDA Import Systems. Cancelled CBP entries display static
text at the top of multiple screens that reads, "This Entry is Cancelled." The Entry Review Grab Bag (ERGB)

6.2.2.2.6 – Partner/Other Government Agency (OGA) Referral
Use the OGI (Other Government Agency - Investigations) and OGC (Other Government Agency - Compliance)
work types to close an entry line without the FDA making an admissibility decision. This is done when another
government agency is handling the entry.
•
To send a line to the Investigations Branch (IB): Select "OGI/OGA" from the "Possible Actions." This
allows the IB to close the line with no further FDA action.
•
To send a line to the Compliance Branch (CB): Select "OGC/OGA" from the "Possible Actions." This
routes the line to the CB and allows them to close it with no further FDA action.
When to Use an OGA Referral to Close a Line
You can close a line with an OGA referral when another agency (like CBP) has decided to refuse or seize a
shipment. Before you close the line, you must obtain and upload documents that show what the other agency
did with the product.
Sharing Information vs. Closing a Line
If you only need to share information with another agency but the FDA will make an initial admissibility
decision, use an Ad-Hoc OGA Referral (an Ad-Hoc referral does not close the line).
Handling Cancelled Entries
If CBP cancels an entry, the entry is handled in one of two ways:
•
No Work Assigned: The system will automatically close the entry.
•
Work Assigned: You must email OII-OBISM-DIBIS-IMPORT-APPS-PROBLEM-REPORTS@fda.hhs.gov to
request that they close the cancelled entry.
Requesting a New OGA
If you need to refer an entry to an agency that is not listed in the system, submit your request using the Import
Support Form.

Request documents using the "Documents Required" Entry Option (DRQ) when you need additional information to

6.2.3.2.1 – Uploading documents received outside of ITACS
If you receive documents for an entry outside of ITACS (for example, by email), you must upload them into the

You should upload documents such as:

Product labeling
•
Email conversations that could affect admissibility
•
Entry paperwork like invoices, packing slips, or official forms
Note: You can also upload screenshots of web pages. Always follow the official record retention policy for all

6.2.3.3 – Review of Entry Documents
Review documents in the order they arrive.
After reviewing the documents, decide which action to take next.
•
If you find evidence of a violation (or cannot confirm compliance), send a Detention Request (DTR) to the
Compliance Branch.
•
If an examination or sample collection is indicated, set up a work request (e.g., LEX, FEX, or SAM).
If the documents are not sufficient, you have three ways to follow up with the filer:
1. Direct Communication: Email or call the filer.
2. DEF Option: Use the "Entry Incomplete - Return, Deficient Entry" option.
3. INF Activity: Use the "Request Information" activity.
In your message, clearly state the specific information you need and let the filer know that the admissibility review
will proceed without it if not provided.
If you do not receive the requested documents within three business days, the system will automatically send a
"Second and Final Request" (DR2) to the filer. Note: This is an electronic message, not a formal "Notice of FDA
Action."
Documenting Your Communication
•
Direct Calls/Emails: Log all direct communications in the "Remarks" field or use the "Log Miscellaneous
Information" (MIB) function. Include the date, contact person, information requested, and your initials.
•
DEF and INF: These options generate a formal "Notice of FDA Action." You must write the details of your
request in the "Narrative" or "Information Requested" field.
•
Note: narrative field appears on the public "Notice of FDA Action".
6.2.4 – Conditional Release Period
Customs and Border Protection (CBP) may conditionally release a product while it awaits an FDA admissibility decision.
You must make this decision before the conditional release period ends.
This period typically lasts for 30 days. It ends as soon as one of these things happens first:
•
The FDA issues a "May Proceed" notice.
•
The FDA issues a notice of refusal.
•
The 30-day period expires.
How to Extend the Period
To extend the conditional release period, you must notify the importer in writing within the first 30 days. Your notice
must state that the FDA will examine, sample, or detain the product.
Important: The following actions do not extend the conditional release period:
•
Requesting Documents (DRQ)
•
Detention Requests (DTR or DER)
•
Deficient Entry (DEF)
•
Requesting Information (INF)
[p.21]









6.2.5 - Detention Recommendations by Entry Reviewers
If a product appears non-compliant, you have two options:
1. Request field work.
2. Recommend detention to the Compliance Branch (CB).

6.2.5.1 - Submission of Detention Recommendations to the Compliance Branch
As an entry reviewer, you will use one of two work types to recommend detention: DER or DTR.
DER (Detention without Physical Examination): Use DER when a product is subject to Detention without Physical
Examination (DWPE).
DTR (All Other Detentions): Use DTR for all other situations where a product appears to be violative.
Before sending the detention request to the Compliance Branch (CB), you must:
•
Gather all necessary documents to support your case.
•
Add clear comments explaining the reason for detention for each line.
•
Include photos when needed to support the violation.
6.2.5.2 - General Procedures Pertaining to all Detention Recommendations (DER and DTR)
Before you submit a detention recommendation, you must complete the following steps to ensure it is accurate
and aligns with FDA requirements.
Check the Import Program page for any product-specific instructions. Then, search all relevant center databases.
Important: You must document all the research you conduct in the detention recommendation's "Remarks"
section.
Note: All information in "Line Details" must be accurate. Inaccurate data will affect PREDICT screening.

If a product has multiple violations (for example, it is subject to more than one Import Alert), you must include all
applicable alerts and problem area flags (PAFs) in your detention recommendation.
6.2.5.2.1 – Obtaining Entry Documents for Compliance Review
Entry documents are needed for the Compliance Officer (CO) to review the case. If you do not have the
documents, follow these steps.
When you select the detention option (DER or DTR), choose an "Entry Option" from the drop-down menu that
also requests documents.
For example, select "Hold Designated, Others Go, Docs Required." This automatically tells the filer to submit
the needed paperwork.
If you receive documents outside of ITACS, you must upload them into the FDA Import system.
Refer to these guides for specific instructions on how to upload documents:
•
Job Aid for the Entry Review Application
•
SERIO User Manual
6.2.5.3 - DER - Import Alert (IA)
Use a DER when an entry is subject to DWPE.
You can submit a DER to the Compliance Branch (CB) without requesting entry documents if all the following are
true:
•
The electronic data matches the Import Alert criteria (e.g., country, manufacturer, product).
•
The Import Alert does not require specific documents.
•
You do not need any additional information.
When You MUST Request Documents First
You must request and review documents before submitting a DER if the Import Alert requires specific paperwork
for a product to be released.
[p.22]









For example, IA 28-02 requires a certificate for all shipments of black pepper from India. If the certificate is
missing, you must request it before recommending detention.
How to Submit a DER
1. Confirm that all the electronic information (country, firm name, product description) matches the criteria
in the Import Alert. Update and rescreen the entry if you make corrections.
2. In the "Instruction Text" field, explain why you are recommending detention. For example: "Manufacturer
is subject to IA XX-XX." Also, document any database research you performed if the IA requires it.
3. Submit to the Compliance Branch (CB).
6.2.5.4 - DTR
Use a DTR when you cannot confirm that a product meets the FDA's admissibility requirements.
To make this decision, first review all available information, including:
•
The electronic submission
•
Internal FDA databases
•
Any documents submitted by the filer
If you still need more information, you can also request a field exam or sample collection.
6.2.5.4.1 - Similar to Import Alert Listing
If an entry line appears to match an Import Alert (IA) listing, but you need more information to be sure, follow
these steps.
First, request and review the entry documents. If you find any errors in the electronic data, correct them and
rescreen the line.
Determine the Next Step
•
If the entry line is matches an IA listing (red/green/yellow list) follow the guidance section in the IA.
•
If the entry line does not match the IA listing:
•
Continue your initial admissibility review.
•
Using the feedback functionality in SERIO Entry Review, explain why it was not subject to the
IA. For example: "Flagged for IA XX-XX, but manufacturer not listed on IA".
6.2.5.4.2 – Pending Addition to DWPE
As an entry reviewer, you may find entries with the same product and manufacturer as a previous violative
entry. That previous entry may be pending addition to DWPE.
What you do next depends on if OII has direct reference authority.
NOTE: The CO may set up screening criteria in these cases. This helps reviewers know about violative findings.
6.2.5.4.2.1 Direct Reference Authority for DWPE
When OII has direct reference authority (DIO Advisory #1) AND the electronic entry exactly matches a
previous violative entry.
When you encounter this:
1. Recommend Detention without physical examination (DER).
2. Add comments in the "Instruction Text" field (found in Work Details > Work Request and Work
Request Details screen). Include:
o
Previous violative findings
o
CMS/work activity number and/or entry number
o
Reference to the IA
o
Any evidence you collected
6.2.5.4.2.2 - No Direct Reference Authority for DWPE
When OII does not have direct reference authority, the entry must stand on its own.
Discuss next steps with your supervisor and CB. Possible next steps could include the following:
•
Request and/or review entry documents.
•
Request Field Work (via SAM/FEX/LEX).
